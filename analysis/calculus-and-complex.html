Analysis

Calculus on the line, then a first look at \(\mathbb C\)

Analysis is the study of limits, and of the operations that survive them: derivatives, integrals, infinite sums. The three galleries below, from @eeanimation, are the introduction to this page: branches of the subject, the standard calculus theorems, then complex analysis in more detail.

1 Branches of mathematical analysis

Real analysis builds the foundations. Complex analysis studies functions of \(z = x+iy\). Functional analysis moves to infinite dimensions; harmonic analysis reads frequency; numerical and asymptotic analysis approximate; variational and nonlinear analysis handle extrema and \(F(x)=0\).

23 August 2026.

2 Calculus theorems

A useful habit: every interchange of a limit and an operation is a theorem. The series runs from the squeeze theorem through the divergence theorem — hypotheses first, formula second.

12 September 2026.

3 Complex analysis

A function \(f:\Omega\subset\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\) is holomorphic if it is complex-differentiable on an open set. That single requirement is much stronger than real differentiability in two variables: Cauchy–Riemann, power series, contour integrals, residues, conformal maps.

6 September 2026.

4 Bridges to the rest of the site

  • Linear ODEs with constant coefficients are solved by characteristic polynomials — that is linear algebra over \(\mathbb C\).
  • Generating functions and characteristic functions in probability are analytic objects first, probabilistic ones second.
  • Hamilton–Jacobi theory on the dynamics page is a first-order nonlinear PDE.
