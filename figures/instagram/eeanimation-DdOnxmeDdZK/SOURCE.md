## Caption

Markov chains can be classified by how time evolves, the size of their state space, their transition structure, and whether their states are directly observable.

A chain may operate in discrete or continuous time, contain finite or countably infinite states, include absorbing states, or be irreducible and ergodic. Hidden Markov models add another layer: the underlying states are not observed directly but produce measurable outputs.

These categories can overlap—a discrete-time chain, for example, may also be finite-state and absorbing.
