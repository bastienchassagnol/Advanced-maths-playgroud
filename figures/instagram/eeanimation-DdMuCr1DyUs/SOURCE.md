Calculus is held together by a remarkable collection of theorems connecting limits, continuity, derivatives, integrals, geometry, and vector fields.

The series covers:

1. Squeeze Theorem
2. Intermediate Value Theorem
3. Extreme Value Theorem
4. Fermat’s Theorem
5. Rolle’s Theorem
6. Mean Value Theorem
7. Cauchy’s Mean Value Theorem
8. Taylor’s Theorem
9. L’Hôpital’s Rule
10. Inverse Function Theorem
11. Implicit Function Theorem
12. Clairaut’s Theorem
13. Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
14. Integration by Parts
15. Leibniz Integral Rule
16. Fubini’s Theorem
17. Green’s Theorem
18. Stokes’ Theorem
19. Divergence Theorem

Together, these results explain when limits exist, why extrema occur, how local change predicts global behavior, how differentiation and integration undo one another, and how integrals over boundaries relate to quantities inside them.

The hypotheses matter just as much as the formulas: every theorem is a precise statement of what becomes guaranteed when its assumptions are satisfied.
