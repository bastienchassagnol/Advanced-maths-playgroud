Linear algebra

Vectors, products, inverses, and the spectrum

A decomposition is a rewrite \(A = \cdots\) that makes a computation, or a geometric fact, cheap. Before the factorisations, the objects being multiplied have to be named. The galleries below are from @eeanimation.

1 Introduction to vectors

A vector is an element of a vector space: you can add two and scale one, and you stay in the space. Arrows in the plane are the first picture, not the definition. Functions, polynomials, and matrices can be vectors too.

Ten-part series, 7 September 2026.

2 Vector multiplications

There is no single “times” for vectors. The symbol decides the output: a scalar (dot), a perpendicular vector (cross), a matrix (outer), a bivector (wedge), or a multivector (geometric product).

14 September 2026.

3 Methods of matrix inversion

There is no single best inverse. Gauss–Jordan is pedagogical; adjugate is for small symbolic work; LU, QR, Cholesky, and SVD are the numerical defaults. Forming \(A^{-1}\) is often the wrong move: solve \(Ax=b\) with a factorisation instead.

4 September 2026.

4 Eigenvalue algorithms

\(A v = \lambda v\) is the decomposition \(A = X\Lambda X^{-1}\) when \(A\) is diagonalisable. Which algorithm you want depends on whether \(A\) is sparse or symmetric, and on whether you need one eigenpair or the whole spectrum. Do not form \(\det(A-\lambda I)\) as a numerical primitive.

6 September 2026.

5 Factorisations by job

Decomposition Shape / hypotheses Typical use
LU (with pivoting) Square, generic Solve \(Ax=b\); determinant
QR Full column rank Least squares; orthonormal bases
Cholesky Hermitian positive definite Fast \(Ax=b\); covariance
Eigen \(A = X\Lambda X^{-1}\) Square, diagonalisable Powers, linear ODEs \(\dot x = Ax\)
Spectral \(A = Q\Lambda Q^\top\) Symmetric / Hermitian Axes of a quadratic form
SVD \(A = U\Sigma V^\top\) Any Rank, condition, PCA, low-rank approx.
Polar Square, invertible Closest rotation times stretch
Schur Square Numerically stable “eigen” form
Jordan Square, over \(\mathbb C\) Theoretical canonical form (unstable)

After you linearise a vector field (differential equations), the local picture is \(\dot x = Ax\). The eigen- or Schur form of \(A\) is the dynamical classification.
